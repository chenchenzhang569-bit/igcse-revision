Stem and Leaf diagrams

Definition

  • Stem-and-Leaf diagram is a quick way of summarizing a range of data.

  • There is a column known as the stem, which contains unique elements of data formed by removing last digits of the data.

  • Keys are used in this diagram

Structure

  • A key or legend is always included to show how to read the diagram.

How to find the median of a Stem leaf diagram

  • First ensure the data is ordered from smallest to largest .

  • Locate the middle value.

  • If there's an odd number of data points, the median is the single middle value.

  • If there's an even number of data points, the median is the average of the two middle values

Scatter Diagrams

  • Displays the correlation between two sets of data

  • May have positive, negative or no correlation

  • Scatter graphs are used to plot pairs of data

  • The vertical and horizontal axes represent the two quantities being measured

  • Points are plotted as crosses, ×

  • The general shape formed by the points shows the type of correlation

  • Correlation

  • Positive: Indicates a relationship between two variables where they tend to move in the same direction

  • Negative: An inverse relationship between two variables, meaning as one variable increases, the other decreases

  • No Correlation: No derivable relationship between the two sets of data being plotted

  • Line of Best Fit

  • Use a transparent ruler to visually estimate the trend of the data points

  • Draw a straight line that represents that trend.

  • Aim for roughly the same number of data points above and below the line

Bar Charts

  • Used to represent discrete data (data in separate categories).

  • Each bar represents a category and its frequency (or value).

  • Bars must be of equal width and equally spaced

Key Features

  • Horizontal Axis shows categories

  • Vertical axis shows frequency or measured value

  • Height of Bar = Frequency

  • Used when data is categorical and discrete

Reading a Bar Chart

  • Look at the height of each bar

  • Match it with the scale on the y-axis

  • Compare bars to see the highest, lowest, or differences
